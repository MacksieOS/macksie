# Macksie Accords — DRAFT

> **Status: draft for discussion. Not adopted or ratified.**
> This file organizes future work on the Accords. It does not establish a constitution, finalize governance, or supply the Ten Foundations.

## Purpose

The Accords are intended to describe the commitments that guide Macksie and how people work together. This draft gives contributors a place to identify questions and review proposed wording without presenting unfinished ideas as binding rules.

The project mission is **“Building a free and open internet, together.”** The [README](../README.md) introduces the project; the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) describes the current repository workflow.

## Ten Foundations — authoritative text pending

**The Ten Foundations have deliberately not been written, numbered, reconstructed, or finalized here.**

Their authoritative wording must be supplied and explicitly reviewed. This document contains no replacement principles or inferred list. Neither the questions below nor the eight Foundry Glances are the Ten Foundations.

## Questions for a future draft

These are discussion prompts, not adopted commitments:

- What is the authoritative wording and source of the Ten Foundations?
- What belongs in the Accords, and what belongs in changeable operating procedures?
- How should the Accords relate to the Foundry, Forge, Commons, and shared Core?
- How should established governance decisions be accurately represented, and which details remain unresolved?
- How will people propose changes, review disagreements, and record the rationale for decisions?
- What process and participants can approve an Accords version, and how will that approval be recorded?
- How will versions, amendments, and unresolved objections remain visible to contributors?

## Proposed drafting process

1. Gather authoritative project decisions and source text, distinguishing them from suggestions and historical drafts.
2. Introduce proposed wording through a dedicated pull request with its source, rationale, and open questions.
3. Invite review and record disagreements rather than silently resolving them in prose.
4. Establish and document the approval process before describing any version as adopted.
5. Record an adopted version, date, and decision reference only after that process has actually occurred.

Merging a documentation change does not itself ratify the Accords. Keep the draft banner until an explicit adoption decision is documented through the agreed process. This file does not override prior project decisions or claim that an approval body has already been constituted here.
