# The Macksie Foundry

The Foundry is the organizing home for building Macksie together. It connects planning, architecture, discussion, care, resources, contributors, and practical review so that the project can move from ideas to useful work.

Its eight **Foundry Glances** are **Time, Architects, Roundtable, Sentinel, Benevolence, Treasure, Talent, and Looking Glass**.

**Status:** the names are the requested organizing structure. The responsibilities below are proposed starting descriptions for review, not finalized mandates, an adopted governance charter, or implemented applications. A Glance here is a view of the work; all eight can begin as documentation and coordinated tasks.

## Time

Make priorities, dependencies, milestones, and project history understandable. Keep plans tied to actual progress and distinguish estimates from commitments.

**First contribution:** maintain the [Commons-first roadmap](../docs/ROADMAP.md), recording milestone evidence and unresolved dependencies without inventing release dates.

## Architects

Bring builders and design thinking together around a coherent system. Explore how the Commons, Forge, and shared Core fit together, document alternatives, and keep interfaces proportionate to demonstrated needs.

**First contribution:** review the Commons entry model and the minimum shared capabilities needed for one complete prototype journey.

## Roundtable

Provide a place to discuss proposals, record decisions and disagreements, and make governance questions visible. This description does not create voting rules, assign seats, or replace established governance decisions. Those details need authoritative sources and explicit review.

**First contribution:** track open questions in the [draft Accords](../docs/ACCORDS.md) and document how proposals will be reviewed. Do not supply or finalize the Ten Foundations.

## Sentinel

Focus attention on security, privacy, reliability, and abuse risks. Help contributors understand boundaries and bring evidence to readiness reviews.

**First contribution:** review public knowledge-entry permissions, source handling, revision integrity, and the reporting needs of a future Commons pilot.

## Benevolence

Keep people's needs, accessibility, inclusion, and community benefit visible in project choices. Help identify who a design leaves out and how participation can become easier.

**First contribution:** review the reading and correction journey for clarity and accessibility, and identify ways people can contribute without technical experience.

## Treasure

Make resource needs, costs, and sustainability questions understandable. Document assumptions and proposed budgets so people can review them.

**First contribution:** identify the prototype's likely hosting, storage, and maintenance needs without committing funds, setting fees, or defining financial authority.

## Talent

Help people find useful work, share knowledge, and grow their abilities. Connect contributor interests with scoped tasks, support onboarding, and recognize contributions.

**First contribution:** keep the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) useful for developers, designers, researchers, and volunteers, and propose approachable starter tasks.

## Looking Glass

Make the project's ideas, experiments, repository work, and progress inspectable. Bring together research, prototypes, demonstrations, and review evidence so people can see what exists and what is still proposed.

**First contribution:** organize review of this documentation foundation and later Commons demonstrations, linking questions and findings to issues and pull requests. Keep implementation claims backed by observable results.

## Working together

A Commons task may benefit from several Glances: Time tracks its place in the roadmap, Architects review its design, researchers and Looking Glass gather evidence, Sentinel examines risks, and Benevolence checks accessibility. Talent helps people participate, Treasure makes resource needs visible, and Roundtable provides a place for recorded discussion and decisions.

These perspectives do not imply eight approval gates for every change. Keep work small, record the reasoning that matters, and use the repository's review workflow. The immediate aim is a working Commons prototype, not building every Foundry tool first.
